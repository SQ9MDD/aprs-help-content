APRS is much more than dots moving on a map.

It is a radio-based information network that allows amateur radio operators to see where other stations are, exchange short messages, transmit telemetry, weather data, objects and local information.

## APRS in practice

In real use, APRS can be extremely useful.

You can see that another amateur radio operator is only a few kilometres away and immediately find out which frequency to call them on. You can follow friends during a trip, a mountain activity or an outing on the water. You can receive local information, observe radio traffic in your area, or build your own iGate, digipeater or weather station.

Most importantly, much of this can work directly over radio, without a mobile network and without a central server between users.

APRS combines position, radio and information into one system.

If you think APRS is only about position tracking, you have probably seen only a small part of what it can do.
